# Visual concepts

Use visual concepts to resolve a design decision, not as decoration. Decide the number of concepts or section frames from the request; do not impose a fixed grid, screen count, or style.

For each concept, specify audience, message, conversion goal, composition, type hierarchy, palette, image treatment, interaction cues, and the real implementation constraint it is testing. Distinct concepts should differ in design logic, not just color.

When generating section frames or assets:

- match aspect ratio and crop to the intended placement;
- maintain one identity across a multi-section set;
- keep required text short and legible, and provide exact copy separately when image text may be unreliable;
- avoid generic filler imagery and repeated use of one crop;
- label concepts or sections outside the artwork when labels would pollute the design;
- inspect every generated result and retry only failed, inconsistent, or unreadable outputs.

Hand implementation a short design token summary and asset map so the final page can reproduce the chosen direction rather than approximating it from memory.

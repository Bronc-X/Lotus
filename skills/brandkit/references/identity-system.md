# Identity system

Translate the brief into a compact system:

1. Brand premise: audience, problem, promise, personality, proof, and contexts where the identity appears.
2. Concept spine: one metaphor and a short explanation of why it fits.
3. Mark: symbol, wordmark, lockup, clear space, minimum size, monochrome behavior, and light/dark variants.
4. Palette: primary, supporting, accent, and neutral roles with accessible combinations.
5. Typography: display and text roles, hierarchy, weights, and practical fallbacks.
6. Supporting language: shape, grid, line, pattern, material, photography, illustration, and motion principles only when useful.
7. Applications: choose the smallest set that proves the system in the user’s actual contexts.

For multiple concepts, vary the central metaphor and design logic, not just color. Explain tradeoffs in memorability, tone, scalability, and implementation. Avoid generic technology symbols, copied marks, random gradients, decorative pseudo-meaning, and invented brand claims.

Treat AI-generated text inside images as provisional. Deliver exact names, taglines, color values, and typography guidance separately when precision matters.
